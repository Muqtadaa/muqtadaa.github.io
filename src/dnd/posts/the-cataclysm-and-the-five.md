Every homebrew world needs one event that explains why the ruins are better than
the cities. Agdaron has the Cataclysm.

It is a thousand years in the past, it killed most of the pantheon, and the
party has spent years of real time finding out that almost nothing they were
told about it is complete.

## Before

The gods came to Agdaron as a family, into a world of violent seas and mountains
still heaving themselves out of the earth, shared with Primordial Spirits that
wore fire, stone, wind and water as flesh. For a while the gods worked together,
carving order out of chaos and making a place where lesser things could live.
The mortal races came out of that work.

So did the first great mistake.

Althea, Goddess of Light, gave mortals magic.

The age that followed is the one nobody alive can reproduce. Towers went up by
spellcraft. Impossible machines crossed mountains and seas. Artificers worked
beside priests and wizards on wonders that modern Agdaron now attributes to the
gods, because the alternative — that people did it — is harder to believe.

## The wound

The gift that raised mortals so high taught them to look higher. Mages began
reaching for immortality and apotheosis, and their experiments tore open the
boundaries of the world.

Things from beyond the ordered planes came through the Gates.

The gods went to war with the Outsiders and barely won. Civilisations broke.
Whole regions were left warped by uncontrolled magic, and still are. A pantheon
of many gods — belonging to every race and every alignment — was cut down to
five.

Althea ended it. She spent her own divine essence destroying the Outsiders
inside Agdaron and sealing the Gates behind them, and in doing so was stripped
of her True Name. The histories do not record who she was. They record only that
there was once a goddess of light, and that she is no more.

The surviving gods withdrew from direct interference in mortal affairs and have
kept to that for a thousand years. Their worshippers rebuilt among ruins full of
relics from an age greater than their own.

## The five who are left

| God | Domain |
|---|---|
| Aphiel | Life. A mortal hero who ascended; relics he carried as a man still turn up. |
| Uphia | Death. |
| Morudin | The Great Mountain Forge. The dwarves' god, and the one who answers oaths sworn on steel. |
| Melorah | Nature. |
| Malyr | Battle and Might — and, in the half of the creed his church prefers to forget, Justice and Defence. |

And the sixth entry, which every temple in Agdaron writes the same way:
*Unknown — Goddess of the Light.*

Not every casualty of the Cataclysm is unnamed. **Arathis**, goddess of the
elves, died in it, which is why the Church District of Elleris stands mostly
empty. **Corellon**, the elven god from whose blood the eladrin first arose, has
a tomb, and the party has been inside it.

## True Names, and the hearts of gods

Two pieces of divine mechanics matter more than the rest of the theology
combined, because both of them have shown up at the table.

A god's **True Name** is not a label. It is the thing the god is. Strip it and
you do not get a corpse; you get something that persists without being able to
be anything, which is its own kind of sentence.

A **Divine Heart** — a God's Heart, if you are a dwarf — is a crystalline relic
left where a god fell. The dwarves dug one out from beneath the Barazig Spires
while excavating, which went about as well as that sentence suggests. There are
more of them, people are collecting them, and not all of those people are alive.

## Faith, in the absence of answers

No god has answered anyone directly in a thousand years, which leaves churches
to speak on their behalf. That gap is where most of Agdaron's politics lives.

The **Church of Malyr** rules Malinar in all but name, and has spent the peace
accumulating the sort of authority that armies notice. It preaches Battle and
Might. It is much quieter about Justice and Defence.

The **Path of Peace** is the other major tradition, a monotheist faith that
folds the named gods into aspects of a single divine — Malah, goddess of nature,
and Morudin, god of the mountain, among them. It is followed widely enough that
you will hear both names for the same figure depending on which end of a road
you are standing on.

Magic today is a fraction of what it was before the Cataclysm. Everyone knows
it. Every wizard who has ever stood in a pre-Cataclysm ruin has had the same
thought as the mages who caused the disaster, which is the whole problem.
