Agdaron started as a map. Before there was a campaign, a pantheon or a single
named NPC, there was a sheet of parchment-coloured canvas in Procreate with a
coastline, a mountain range that took a whole evening, and a compass rose I was
far too proud of. The world grew outward from wherever the party decided to walk
next, which is why the interesting parts are all clustered along one road.

What follows is the version a traveller could piece together from tavern talk
and a decent atlas. The things nobody is supposed to know are in
[the campaign](/dnd/the-undying-of-the-light/).

## Two kingdoms and a bad twenty years

The Eastern Continent is dominated by two powers that share a border and very
little else.

**Elleria** is the elven kingdom, ruled by a High King from the City of Elleris,
and it reaches the sea through the Falliel Bay in its southeast. **Malinar** is
the human kingdom, closer to the coast, and moves its trade through ports along
the Rovari.

They spent nearly twenty years killing each other. It started as a border
dispute — armed forces came to blows at roughly the same moment as a major
political gaffe — and it never really stopped being about land and so-called
honour. The war left the rich richer and the poor poorer. Elleria's middle class
shrank to a fraction of its old size and has not grown back.

The fighting ended with the Tofordian Treaty, negotiated by the Halfling Council
of Elders because neither crown would sit in the other's capital. Trade caravans
run the agreed routes again. Nobody trusts anybody, so every caravan travels
with soldiers or mercenaries.

Outside the two kingdoms, the wild lands are exactly what the phrase promises:
monsters, bandits, brigands and the sort of adventure that gets people killed.

## The City of Elleris

Elleris is a beautiful city if you stay above ground, which is the sort of
sentence that should worry you.

The walls are tall and the streets are cobblestone, whorled with colour. The
Lower District is trade — merchants, blacksmiths, food markets and a great many
pubs. The Middle District is where common elves and everyone else actually live,
along with the public schools and most of the entertainment. The Upper District
belongs to high elves, private academies and expensive shops. The Court District
holds the diplomats, the nobles and the High King.

The Church District holds small temples to the gods and almost nobody visits
them. The elven goddess Arathis died in the Cataclysm a thousand years ago, and
the elves have never quite forgiven the divine for it.

Castle Elleric sits at the southwest of the kingdom: five towers on a pentagonal
inner wall, brickwork and vinery swirling up them in colours that should not
work together, domes on top, white marble and gold filigree inside. It is the
prettiest building in the world and the party escaped it through the sewers.

Beneath all of it are the Cisterns — a wandering maze of pipes, sewers and
tunnels that leads out of the city in a dozen directions. This is where the
campaign actually begins.

## The mountains

Northwest of the kingdoms are the **Barazig Spires**, magical mountains holding
three of the five dwarven Underkeeps. Barazar is the great one; Frostspire lies
to its northwest and Iron Deep to its southwest, and the road splits between
them with well-kept torches burning along the stone.

Barazar is a city inside a mountain, and the dwarves have not been subtle about
it. The approach runs through a working quarry and onto cobblestone laid with
King Aghat Umraz's heraldry. Bronze, silver and gold automatons stand guard at
regular intervals, humanoid from the waist up and rolling on a single sphere.
Rail carts carry you inside, driven by a conductor with a hand on a crystal.

The Market district beneath the mountain has a ceiling so high it passes for a
night sky, lit by drifting mage-lights and one brighter light that stands in for
the sun at about half strength. Stay long enough and the under-dwelling madness
starts working on you anyway.

## The neighbours

- **North** — the Frostwind Knolls, home to the Frost Giants, and past them the
  Frozen Wastes and the Hoarfrost Waters.
- **West** — the Ligonqui Settlements, where the Aasimar live. Further west is
  Logaara, where the last of the Orcish Encampments hold on.
- **Southwest** — Toford, the halfling holes, including Beyhearth and Ashbluff.
  The Council of Elders there brokered the treaty that ended the war.
- **South** — across the Falliel Bay, the **Republic of Dolerith**, a human
  democracy, with Dolonde as its capital and Dryar as its second city.
- **Southeast** — the Bright Sahra and its mountains, homeland of the Tabaxi.
- **East and northeast** — the Rovari Sea, where pirates are known to lie in
  wait, and Serpents Bay beyond it.

Malinari cities are laid out in a grid rather than grown into one: a Temple
District for holy power, a Court District for the legislature, an Army District
for the barracks and training grounds, a Middle District of good shops and
schools, and a Lower District of bazaars and travellers that the guard patrols
less than it should. Part of that last one is unofficially outside the city
gates altogether, including the Dragon and Badger Inn.

## The edges of the map

Across the Rovari is the **Western Continent**, which the Cataclysm mostly
destroyed. Very little should be able to live there. Whatever does must be
strange indeed.

The **Far West** of the Eastern Continent is largely unexplored. Its jungles,
forests, deserts and tundra are saturated with wild magic, and the beasts that
live in them have been warped by the magical backlash of the Cataclysm. Agdaron
keeps a guarded border along the Western Forests and leaves the rest alone.

Beyond those forests, across the remainder of the continent, lies the Evatnian
Ocean. None of the living races have sailed it in a thousand years.
