The Stonehearts had been someone once. Ten floors of manor carved into the
mountain wall by the founders of Barazar, and a sword that meant something when
it was drawn.

By Belediin's generation the manor was sold and the family lived in a
three-storey townhouse in the common residential district. The sword was a story
his grandfather told. The dwarf leading the merchant caravan out of Elleris
called him "my lord" and meant it as an insult.

Belediin went looking for the blade.

## What he is good at

Standing where the party needs someone to stand. He is not fast — the rogues and
the wizard leave him behind in every chase the campaign has ever run — but very
little gets past him, and the things that try tend to regret it. He has an
affinity for stone that has talked him through more than one wall.

The Sword of the Stonehearts came back to him in the ruins of Caer Morghaen. Its
sheath did not; that went north on the back of a possessed dwarven child. What
the blade does, it does through him: when he calls up Spirit Guardians at full
strength, what answers is the shade of the great-grandfather who lost the sword
in the first place.

He also carries a third of the Masterwork of Morudin, unshielded, which has been
quietly working on his body for years.

## What it has cost

More than anyone else at the table.

He met Arielle Coldaine in a vision before he ever met her in the flesh — a mute
dwarven girl in great distress, wearing his family's crest on a sheath across
her back. He comforted her. Weeks later he watched a fallen goddess open her eyes
from inside that child's body, and he has been trying to get her out ever since.

He contracted vampirism on a mountain Althea raised beneath his feet in Dolonde,
built himself a coffin to sleep in, and spent a season in the Feywild bargaining
with fey royalty for a cure. He got one.

His father is not missing. His father fell through the Ruby Gate into the Astral
Sea and is a Devil Prince in Hell. His mother is imprisoned there.

Belediin is on his way down.

## The oath

In a temple in Dolonde, with nothing to offer but his own body, he knelt and
swore to Morudin that he would accept any affliction laid upon him so long as it
did not leave his friends or the world weaker for his weakness — and that he
would carry the Stonesword against every wrong he had witnessed, in righteous
dwarven fury, for as long as he could lift it.

Morudin, by all available evidence, was pleased.

## Where he is now

Aboard the Gray Lady's Peril, descending toward a mine in the eighth layer of
Hell where a devil lord is waiting who used to be his father.
