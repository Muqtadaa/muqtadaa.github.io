Of the six who left Elleris, Aedin was the only one not running from something.

He was in the Temple of Aphiel talking with its leader, Ophelia Urea, about
where he had been and whom he had helped, and she asked what she always asked:
whether he had found a new group to travel with.

He had not. Then he felt the pull.

The records call him a humble and well-meaning life-keeper of the god of Light
and Life, and a warm soul. He stood at the back with a spiritual weapon and kept
the others standing. In the cisterns beneath Elleris he beat at an angry spirit
while Nerissa worked a lock, and in every fight afterwards he did the same job
without complaint.

At Barazar the party split across the rail carts that run through the mountain.

Aedin took the wrong one.

It went deep into the belly of the Spires. Nobody noticed in time. He is not
dead, so far as anyone has been able to prove. He never came back up.

Nerissa found his teacher afterwards and told her what had happened. She was
saddened. She was not surprised.

The Amulet of Aphiel, carried by the god himself before he was a god and
recovered from the cisterns on the party's first night together, travelled on
without him. Years later, above the pirate haven of Leviathan, it cracked down
the middle and went cold, and across the city Aphiel's clergy collapsed where
they stood.

Aedin was not there to see it.
