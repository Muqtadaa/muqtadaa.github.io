Of the six who left Elleris, Aedin is the only one who was not running from
something.

He was in the Temple of Aphiel talking with its leader, Ophelia Urea, about
where he had been and who he had helped, and she asked the question she always
asked: had he found a new group to travel with yet.

He hadn't. Then he felt the pull.

The records describe him as a humble and well-meaning life-keeper of the god of
Light and Life, and a warm soul. Both descriptions come from the same person and
both are meant kindly. In a fight he stood at the back with a spiritual weapon
and kept everyone else standing; in the cisterns under Elleris he bashed away at
an angry spirit while Nerissa worked a lock, and in every fight after that he
did roughly the same job without complaint.

## What happened

The party reached Barazar, the great dwarven Underkeep, and split across the
rail carts that run through the mountain.

Aedin got onto the wrong one.

It went deep into the belly of the Spires. Nobody noticed until far too late,
and nobody has seen him since. He is not dead, so far as anyone has been able to
prove. He simply never came back up.

Some time afterward, Nerissa found Aedin's teacher and told her what had
happened.

She was saddened.

She was not, in any way, surprised.

## What he left

The Amulet of Aphiel — carried by the god himself before he was a god, recovered
from the cisterns on the party's first night together — travelled on without
him.

Years later, above the pirate haven of Leviathan, it cracked down the middle and
went cold, and across the city Aphiel's clergy collapsed where they stood.

Aedin was not there to see it.

That may be a mercy.
